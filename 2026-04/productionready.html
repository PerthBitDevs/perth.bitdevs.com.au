ProductionReady

A 501(c)(3) funding a "conservative" alternative Bitcoin client

WHAT IS IT
A US public charity launched March 23, 2026. Mission: fund development of an alternative Bitcoin client prioritizing stability, security, and fidelity to Bitcoin's foundational principles. Built on Bitcoin Core's codebase (not a rewrite), differentiating on development process and policy defaults.
THE BOARD
Jimmy Song — Bitcoin developer, author of "Programming Bitcoin"

Samson Mow — CEO of JAN3

Parker Lewis — Author of "Gradually, Then Suddenly"

John W. Ratcliff — Bitcoin developer/advocate
WHAT "CONSERVATIVE" MEANS
Reverses the burden of proof: changes require "overwhelming support" before merging, rather than the current model where changes are accepted unless blocked.

Endgame: protocol ossification — Bitcoin becoming "effectively unchangeable."

Born from the OP_RETURN War

THE TRIGGER
In mid-2025, Bitcoin Core merged removal of the 80-byte OP_RETURN data limit for v30, championed by maintainer Gloria Zhao. Luke Dashjr called it "utter insanity." Node operators migrated en masse to Bitcoin Knots — from roughly 480 to roughly 820 nodes in weeks. Jimmy Song later described Knots share as moving from about 2% to over 15%. Samson Mow publicly declared Core developers had "become a risk to Bitcoin."
POLICY POSITIONS
OP_RETURN: Would restore the data limit to its previous default

BIP-110: "Not expected to be included given the lack of broad user consensus" — more cautious than even some OP_RETURN critics

Same consensus rules as Core — this is a policy fork, not a chain fork. Blocks validate identically.
THE FRAMING
Jimmy Song: "A conservative implementation isn't just a technical preference — it's a statement that Bitcoin belongs to its users." The initiative channels growing sentiment that Core prioritizes developer preferences over network stability. Whether that framing is fair is the debate.

Discussion Points

THE CASE FOR
Implementation diversity reduces single points of failure — no single team dictates protocol direction. The Knots migration showed organic demand for alternatives. Multiple well-maintained implementations could create healthy checks and balances on the development process. The OP_RETURN controversy proved that node operators will vote with their feet.
THE CASE AGAINST
The board is composed of public figures and business leaders, not protocol developers — critics may see governance capture by business interests. Bitcoin Knots already fills the "conservative alternative" niche. Framing Core developers as reckless is contentious — the same developers maintained consensus safety for 16 years. A third client may fragment already-scarce review effort.
THE BIGGER QUESTION
Is this a healthy ecosystem response to legitimate governance concerns, or an escalation of political disputes into technical infrastructure? The OP_RETURN change was a relay policy decision — not a consensus rule change. If policy disagreements spawn competing implementations, what happens when there's an actual consensus debate?
