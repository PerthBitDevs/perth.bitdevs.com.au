One transaction can need many signatures

A transaction spends earlier payments through inputs and creates new payments as outputs.

ALICE AND BOB SPEND TOGETHER
  • Alice and Bob contribute one coin each. Each input needs its owner’s authorisation.
  • Under Bitcoin’s Taproot rules, a simple spend uses a 64-byte Schnorr signature. Two inputs need two signatures.
  • Several signers controlling one coin is multisig. CISA combines authorisations across different input coins.
COMBINE THE SIGNATURE DATA
  • Cross-input signature aggregation (CISA) compresses these signatures into one proof that nodes check.
  • Half-aggregation: combine signatures after people sign separately.
  • Full aggregation: people cooperate while signing to produce a smaller proof.

Draft Bitcoin Improvement Proposals BIPs458–460 specify these schemes and their use in transactions. Published 2–4 November (UTC); not activated on mainnet.

BIP458 · 2 Nov UTC BIP459 · 3 Nov UTC BIP460 · 4 Nov UTC

Two ways to reduce signature data

A Schnorr signature has two 32-byte parts. Ten separate signatures therefore use 640 bytes.

Signature bytes only; transaction framing and BIP460 markers excluded.
MethodTen inputsWhat gets combined
Separate signatures640 bytesTen complete 64-byte signatures.
BIP458: half-aggregation352 bytesKeep each first part; combine the second parts into one value: 10 × 32 + 32.
BIP459: full aggregation64 bytesProduce one signature covering all participating keys and the transaction data each authorises.

Input references, amounts and destinations remain, so percentage fee savings are smaller than signature-byte savings at a fixed fee rate.

Full aggregation requires two signing rounds

DahLIAS, the scheme in BIP459, creates its 64-byte signature through an exchange between the participating wallets.

A coordinator collects the messages; each wallet must still authorise its own spend.
RoundWhat the wallets do
1. PrepareEach wallet creates fresh, single-use signing material called a nonce. It sends the public part and receives the combined result plus everyone’s keys and transaction data.
2. SignEach wallet checks that session and returns a partial signature. The coordinator combines the partials into the final signature.
THE COORDINATION COST
  • A participant can stall the session. Reusing secret nonce material can expose a private key.
WHY KEEP HALF-AGGREGATION?
  • People can sign separately, at different times. Someone combines their completed signatures afterwards.

Aggregation needs a new Bitcoin output type

Each output sets conditions for spending its coins. BIP460 proposes a new type whose conditions allow aggregated signatures.

HOW NODES RECOGNISE THE RULES
  • The spending data, including signatures, is called the witness. A version number on the output tells nodes how to check it.
  • BIP460 proposes witness version 2. Existing Taproot outputs use different rules and would not change automatically.
  • After activation, coins would need to be sent to the new output type to use aggregation.
WHICH SPENDS ARE INCLUDED?
  • A key-path spend authorises payment with a signature. These signatures can aggregate within one transaction.
  • A script-path spend reveals a program with additional spending conditions. Those signatures are excluded.
  • The signer chooses half, full or no aggregation. This preserves protocols that need the exact individual signature.

The draft has no activation schedule, and the proposed version number needs coordination. Aggregation does not hide amounts or transaction links.
