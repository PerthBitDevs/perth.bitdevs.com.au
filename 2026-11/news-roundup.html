Liquid’s 1:1 BTC backing is restored

Liquid is a separate blockchain. Its L-BTC represents BTC in a reserve managed by a group of operators, the Liquid Federation.

Each L-BTC should be backed by one BTC. September’s exploit created unbacked L-BTC that was withdrawn as BTC, leaving roughly 602 BTC missing after a partial return.

BTC IN RESERVE · 3 NOV4,270.54 BTC
L-BTC IN CIRCULATION4,270.54 L-BTC

Reserve top-ups: 2.5 BTC on 27 October, 50 BTC on 28 October, then 300 + 250 BTC on 2 November.

The top-ups total about 602.5 BTC. Federation board member Samson Mow named Blockstream as the funder (The Block, 3 Nov). Liquid nodes can check the proof of reserves.

Liquid withdrawals reopen through SideSwap

Withdrawing BTC from the Federation’s reserve is called a peg-out. That normal route is still restricted.

SideSwap, a Liquid wallet and exchange, reopened conversions on 20 October, paying users from its own BTC wallet:

StepHow the conversion works
1. User sends L-BTCSideSwap receives the user’s L-BTC for conversion to BTC on Bitcoin.
2. SideSwap pays BTCThe payment comes from BTC SideSwap already holds, at 1:1 less its 0.1% fee.
3. SideSwap refillsIt exchanges collected L-BTC for BTC in bulk. Manual refills can leave customer withdrawals waiting in a queue.

Full backing has now been restored. Normal withdrawals from the Federation’s reserves still await the remaining security and operational checks.

BIP139: a shared wallet export format

Changing wallet apps can restore spending keys while losing labels and coin settings. BIP139 proposes a common file for carrying those records across.

INCOMPATIBLE WALLET EXPORTS
  • Labels and spending preferences belong to the app. Recovery words and the blockchain cannot restore all these records.
  • Different export formats can require a custom converter for each pair of apps.
  • Pyth’s draft Bitcoin Improvement Proposal defines shared fields for wallet setup, labels and coin settings. It joined the BIPs repository on 24 October.
EXAMPLE: KEEP A COIN FROZEN
  • In Wallet A, label a coin “invoice 42” and freeze it so the app will not automatically spend it.
  • The export identifies that output and records its label and frozen status.
  • Wallet B could restore those choices if it supports the format and those fields. Unsupported fields are discarded.

Mainnet seed words and secret signing keys are excluded. Keep separate key backups; the exported metadata still exposes financial information.
