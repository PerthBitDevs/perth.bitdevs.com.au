ECX: a separate copy of Bitcoin’s history

ECX forked from Bitcoin at block 973,728 on 29 October: it copied the history and unspent coins, then continued under different rules.

A SEPARATE ASSET AND NETWORK
  • eCash at ecash.com uses ECX. It is separate from Bitcoin and the older eCash project, XEC.
  • Copied Bitcoin outputs become coins on ECX. The fork itself leaves BTC balances unchanged.
  • ECX has its own mining security; copying Bitcoin’s history does not copy its hashpower.
WHAT DRIVECHAINS ENABLE
  • A sidechain is a separate blockchain with its own transaction rules. Drivechains connect ECX to these chains.
  • BIP300: lock ECX, receive a sidechain balance, then ask ECX miners to approve a withdrawal back.
  • BIP301: builders pay ECX miners to record a sidechain block’s cryptographic reference. Miners need not check its transactions.

Peter Todd’s critique of BIP300: miners can approve incorrect withdrawals; the mainchain has no fraud-proof mechanism to overturn them.

ECX reassigns 500,000 copied coins

Spending normally requires the owner’s authorisation. ECX’s new rules let block 973,768 reassign copied balances without it.

ACCEPTED UNDER ECX’S RULES
500,000 ECX
  • 232 transactions sent this amount to 232 distinct addresses.
  • They spent 10,127 copied unspent outputs, the transactions’ inputs, without the original holders’ signatures.
  • The corresponding BTC was not spent.
FUNDING PLAN AND OBJECTION
  • LayerTwo Labs: proposed allocating copied coins it attributed to Satoshi to investors, to finance development and launch.
  • Beau Turner, Abundant Mines: reassigning fork coins violates property rights, even though BTC is untouched.

The transactions show amounts and destinations. They do not establish who owned the original keys or identify the recipients; 232 addresses does not mean 232 investors.

A BTC payment can also move ECX

Replay means copying the same signed transaction to another chain. ECX protection is opt-in.

EXAMPLE: ALICE PAYS BOB
  • An output exists unspent on both Bitcoin and ECX. Alice signs a BTC payment spending it to Bob.
  • Someone copies that transaction to ECX. If valid there too, it transfers Alice’s matching ECX to Bob without another signature.
MAKING THE ECX SPEND DIFFERENT
  • Locktime can mean “not before this block”. ECX accepts the special value 499999998; Bitcoin treats it as a far-future height.
  • Each input has a sequence field. At least one must use a non-final value to enable locktime; otherwise Bitcoin ignores it and the protection fails.

A confirmed protected ECX spend separates only its included coins. Follow wallet-specific guidance and check both chains; a reorganisation can undo a recent split. Never enter a BTC seed or private key into a claiming website.
