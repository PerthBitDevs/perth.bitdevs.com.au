Ledger implants copied recovery phrases

A hardware wallet signs payments inside a separate device. During setup, it displays recovery words that can recreate the spending keys if the device is lost.

EXAMINED NANO X IMPLANTS
  • An added flexible circuit board carried a microcontroller, LTE modem, eSIM and antenna. Wires tapped the screen’s data connection.
  • The microcontroller decoded the recovery words from the screen data. The LTE modem sent them over the cellular network.
LEDGER’S RESELLER ACCOUNT
  • Ledger’s 15 October report: genuine devices were altered, then returned to sale through Southeast Asian resellers.
  • Ledger says its security chip, operating system and servers were not compromised.
  • Resellers must never resell returns, even apparently unopened devices.

The attacker can use copied recovery words to recreate the wallet and spend its funds. Tibane’s examined implants do not establish every affected device’s design.

Ledger response · 15 Oct Tibane’s implant photographs and teardown

A genuine chip can coexist with an implant

Ledger’s genuine check asks whether the device contains an authentic Ledger security chip. It does not inspect every component.

WHAT THE CHECK PROVES
  • The chip has an identity key, separate from the owner’s spending keys. Ledger’s server sends it a random challenge to sign.
  • The server checks the signature. A genuine chip can still answer correctly with an extra circuit fitted beside it.
  • Ledger’s Donjon security team explicitly documents this limitation.
HOW THE EXAMINED IMPLANT SPIED
  • Tibane found a circuit listening to the connection that carries images to the screen.
  • It read the displayed pixels, recognised the recovery words and sent them through its own cellular modem.
  • The implant copied the backup during normal display, without breaking the chip’s protections.

A genuine check or intact packaging cannot rule out added hardware. The teardown covers an examined design, not every affected device.

Binance attackers gained signing access

A hot wallet keeps funds available to an online withdrawal system. Binance’s signing service creates the signatures needed to spend them.

29 October: 18,270 BTC left a Binance hot wallet for an attacker’s address.

DateAccess described in Binance’s report
SeptemberA fake recruitment assignment installs malware on an engineer’s work computer.
20 OctoberAttackers use an internal AI tool to reach other engineers’ computers and steal an access token for installing software.
29 OctoberThey use that token to install malicious code that reaches the signing service, bypassing the normal withdrawal process.

Abusing a signing service can authorise theft without exporting a private key.

Binance’s 3 November findings are preliminary. They do not establish whether key material was also stolen.

Recovery must remove the attacker’s access

Replacing a device or server does not invalidate copied recovery words, passwords or access tokens.
