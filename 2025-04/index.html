The Case for Cross-Input Signature Aggregation (CISA)

Key aggregation combines multiple public keys into a single aggregated public key at address creation time, so a group of signers can produce one joint Schnorr signature. Signature aggregation (CISA) occurs at signing time - taking multiple signatures from different inputs with different public keys and combining them into one. Half aggregation (half-agg) allows signatures to be combined after they are individually produced, without signers needing to interact. Full aggregation (full-agg) requires collaborative signing. CISA itself does not directly improve privacy since all public keys and inputs are still visible.
